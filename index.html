About me

I am an electrical and computer engineer working on non-invasive brain–computer interfaces. For my master’s thesis I built a complete motor-imagery BCI, from EEG recordings with 14 participants to a robotic hand I 3D-printed and assembled myself. I am now a research trainee at g.tec medical engineering, an Austrian medical engineering company that develops brain–computer interfaces, where I build real-time BCI applications. My interest lies where the brain, the human and the robot meet: systems that let people control robots and prostheses with signals from their own body.

Master’s thesis

University of the Peloponnese · Supervisor: Athanasios Koutras, Associate Professor
Graded 10/10

Robotic Arm Control through a Brain–Computer Interface Using EEG Motor Imagery

A brain–computer interface that turns motor imagery into commands for a robotic hand: eight EEG channels, common spatial patterns with a linear classifier, and an Arduino driving the five servos of an InMoov i2 that I printed and assembled. Evaluation holds a whole run out of training, which is the scheme that matches real use.
